Selected Figures
Figure 1. Visualisation of the spatially applied roughness-height distributions on the KCS hull surface.
This graphical representation illustrates the spatial distributions of the equivalent sand-grain roughness height (ks) imposed on the hull surface to assess their hydrodynamic impact. The cases comprise an ideal smooth surface, a spatially uniform roughness field, linear and non-linear distributions, a statistically random distribution, and wall-shear-stress-based gradients prescribed directly and inversely according to the local shear-stress field.
Figure 2. Change in CT relative to uniform roughness for each spatial distribution and fouling level.
Spatial hull roughness leads to pronounced variations in the model-scale total resistance coefficient (CT), with longitudinally varying distributions producing the most significant changes. In particular, linear and non-linear roughness patterns induce substantially larger deviations than the uniform and random cases, whereas the shear-stress-based configurations exhibit relatively minor effects. This behaviour demonstrates the importance of roughness spatiality in model-scale resistance prediction.
Figure 3. Axial velocity contours (Vx/Vship) around the KCS hull, extracted at y = 0.006Lpp, comparing the smooth hull and spatial roughness distributions with the same arithmetic mean roughness height under the B20% condition.
The axial velocity contours show that the spatial distribution of hull roughness alters boundary-layer development and modifies the wake structure. Longitudinally varying roughness patterns produce noticeable changes in boundary-layer growth and wake-deficit formation, whereas the uniform and shear-based configurations produce comparatively milder effects. These flow-field modifications underpin the resistance variations observed in the preceding analysis and highlight the hydrodynamic sensitivity to roughness spatiality.
